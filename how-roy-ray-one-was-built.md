## How it works, in plain words

1. **Start from something proven.** The bot reused the family UNO-style game it had already built, so the rules, scoring and online play were already working.

   This is how agents work fast. They don't start from a blank page. They look back at projects already built, bring in the pieces that already work, like rules, scoring, online play and screen layouts, and spend their time only on what's new: the characters, the branding and the art. Every project makes the next one quicker.
2. **Plan and confirm.** It restated the idea, checked who was Roy and who was Ray, and promised screenshots before anything went public.
3. **Design the look.** Team colors, a globe emblem, the tagline on the home screen, and globe card backs.
4. **Make the art.** Roy and Ray's faces were cut from the real photo for avatars and the Wild cards. Teammates got simple drawn icons. No AI-made pictures of real people.
5. **Build and test.** A helper assistant did the build while the bot kept chatting with Bill, then ran 92 automatic checks on phone-size screens after every change.
6. **Show, then ship.** Bill saw screenshots at each round. Only after he said "Push" did it go live, get re-tested online, and get a QR code.

## What Bill got

**In the game:** Home screen with "Roy & Ray ONE!" and the tagline, 10 selectable players, face cards, solo games, online rooms across phones, badges, and the Golden Globe surprise.

**Behind it:** A single web page that runs on any phone, hosted free on the web, with live online play and a QR code for easy sharing.

## What this means for our team

**Ideas move at the speed of a conversation.** You describe what you want in plain words, react to screenshots, and say go. Reusing what already works, checking before publishing, and testing every change is what made a playable preview in about 17 minutes possible, and the same pattern applies to tools, trackers, and training games for our work.
